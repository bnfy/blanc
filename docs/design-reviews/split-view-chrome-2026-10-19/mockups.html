Split View chrome: three directions

For spec §12 Q1 (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-split-view-design.md). Each direction is shown with two columns and with the 2×2 grid. In every frame the focused pane is the top-left one, “Docs”. Colors, type, the Island and the Glance header come from the app's own CSS; the pages are placeholders.

A. One Island, a header band on every pane chosen 2026-10-19 (spec D5)

The Island stays centered over the whole window and always addresses the focused pane. Every pane, top row included, gets the same 30 px header band at its top, reserved in its geometry and painted by the chrome document.

  • For: one rule everywhere; the Island never moves; every pane can be identified and closed in place; works the same in the grid's bottom row and in every fallback.
  • Against: 30 px of page height per pane, including the top row, where Glance today uses the strip.

B. The Island follows focus (Glance, extended)

The strip band is divided by columns. The focused top-row column holds the Island; every other top-row column shows its header in the strip, exactly like Glance's reference header today. Bottom-row panes get header bands between the views.

  • For: no new chrome for two panes, since it's what Glance users already know; the top row loses no height.
  • Against: the Island jumps between columns when focus moves; the grid's two rows follow different rules; three columns leave little room for the Island in one third of the window.

C. The Island carries the panes, no header bands

No pane headers at all. The Island stays centered and gains a pane switcher: one chip per pane, with the focused pane's chip expanded to its title. The focused pane gets a 2 px top hairline. Back, Reload and the pane menu act on the focused pane from the Island.

  • For: no lost height; closest to Arc, where the split's tabs live in the sidebar and the panes carry almost no chrome.
  • Against: panes can't be identified or closed in place; a pane is closed by focusing it first; the Island gets wider with every pane; the hairline is the only focus cue on the page itself.
