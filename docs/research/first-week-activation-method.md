# First-week activation study — public method

This document is the anonymized, reproducible method for Blanc's Wave 1
activation baseline. Participant identities, contact details, recordings, URLs,
raw notes, and per-person observations remain in the private program folder.

## Sample and release

Recruit 8–12 people across at least three source-browser families, including
macOS and Windows and a mix of new and lightly experienced Blanc users. Record
only a participant code, exact public Blanc version, platform, source-browser
family, and whether the person already uses browser groups or profiles. Do not
record browsing content.

## Fixed task sequence

Each participant receives the same neutral tasks:

1. Bring Favorites or tabs into Blanc.
2. Review what will move before confirming.
3. Explicitly create or edit a Named Group.
4. Quit and reopen Blanc.
5. Predict what will return, then compare the prediction with the result.
6. Explain the difference between a Named Group, local profile, and Named
   Workspace.
7. Explain what optional Sync covers and what it does not.

Moderators do not teach the model before asking for a prediction. They may ask
participants to think aloud, but must not collect credentials, account values,
URLs, or page content. Baseline sessions add no telemetry and no new onboarding
surface.

## Aggregate measures and decision rule

After all sessions, report aggregate task completion, prediction accuracy, and
the number of participants and source-browser families encountering each
blocking friction. Set the first numeric activation target only after the
baseline is complete.

At most one coherent improvement to the existing tour, moving-in checklist, or
Bring Your Tabs flow may advance. It must appear as blocking friction in at
least three sessions or across at least two source-browser families. Any patch
must preserve explicit grouping, separate Favorites and tab-import semantics,
consent-before-measurement, and existing private-data boundaries.

Published results contain sample counts, aggregate findings, limitations, the
numeric target, and the approved product decision. They never contain
participant identities, quotations that identify a person, recordings, URLs,
or individual browsing details.
