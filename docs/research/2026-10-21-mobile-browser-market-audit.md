# Build Blanc for iPhone as a companion

There is room for a mobile Blanc, but only as a small, continuity-first iPhone companion to desktop Blanc. A standalone challenger browser would face a market where Chrome and Safari hold about 92% of worldwide mobile use, and that version fails the ROI case. On iOS, Blanc's strongest desktop advantage, unconstrained network-layer ad blocking, disappears: every iOS browser must use WebKit and Apple's declarative content-blocking API, so Blanc would block about as well as Brave, DuckDuckGo, Orion and Quiche, and no better. What no one offers is the combination Blanc already owns: an open-source (MIT), AI-free, design-led indie browser whose phone app arrives already holding your desktop tabs, Favorites and groups over end-to-end-encrypted sync. Quiche, the strongest indie iOS browser, refuses sync on principle and has no desktop app. Orion is closed source. Vivaldi is a company with a dense UI. The money case is weak. With 43 desktop monthly actives at the last recorded count, a Patron tier that unlocks nothing, and search revenue that no partner pays at indie scale, the base case earns a few hundred dollars a year against roughly six maintainer-months of first-year work. Funding one fifth of a maintainer's time from Patron alone would take something like 47,000 iOS monthly actives. The owner's decision to proceed is defensible on strategic grounds: it completes the product people asked for, it serves desktop retention, and it widens grant eligibility. To pay off, it needs a deliberately thin scope, a pre-committed set of leading indicators, and kill criteria written down before launch.

## Two defaults own 92% of mobile, and iOS removes the engine question

The market is a duopoly with a long, thin tail. In September 2026, StatCounter put Chrome at **67.64%** and Safari at **24.71%** of worldwide mobile browsing. Samsung Internet (3.5%) was the only other browser above 1.5%, and Opera (1.38%), UC (0.78%) and Firefox (0.69%) trailed it ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/mobile/worldwide)). In the US the two leaders are nearly level: Chrome had **47.44%** and Safari **46.38%** in September 2026, the month one tracker called the first time "Chrome passes Safari on US phones" in StatCounter's series ([StatCounter US](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/mobile/united-states-of-america); [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/mobile-browser-usage-statistics/)). On iPhones specifically, Cloudflare measured Safari at **77.8%** and Chrome at 19.1% of iOS traffic in July 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chrome-reaches-66-3-market-share-as-safari-grows-to-15-1-in-q3-2025/)). That leaves every other iOS browser to share a few percent. These figures come from search extracts, because StatCounter's pages could not be fetched, so re-check them before any public use.

The incumbents spent 2026 converging on AI. Microsoft brought Copilot to Edge mobile in May 2026, with multi-tab reasoning and topic-grouped "Journeys" ([Microsoft Edge Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/05/13/new-updates-to-edge-across-desktop-and-mobile/)). Google has promised Gemini in Chrome on iOS without giving a date ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/new-ai-features-for-chrome/)). Perplexity launched Comet on iPhone on March 18, 2026 ([Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/meet-comet-for-ios)), and eWeek reports that Comet collects browsing history to build ad-targeting profiles ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/perplexity-comet-ai-browser-iphone-launch/)). Atlassian paid **$610M** for The Browser Company, while Arc desktop went into maintenance mode and Dia still has no confirmed iOS app ([Wikipedia: Dia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dia_(web_browser))). The opposite stance has a following too. Vivaldi markets itself as "free from generative AI features" ([Vivaldi](https://vivaldi.com/compare/opera/)), and a self-selected DuckDuckGo poll found **90% of about 175,000** respondents didn't want AI in search and browsing ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/12/after-backlash-over-firefox-ai-plans-a-dev-clarifies-users-can-fully-disable-ai-features)). Blanc's marketing-claims rule already forbids AI or agent claims, so the calm, AI-free side is where Blanc belongs.

The most important structural fact is that iOS equalises the engines. Outside the EU, every iOS browser is a WebKit shell, and blocking goes through `WKContentRuleList`. Blanc's own divergence register records that this brings back a Manifest-V3-style rule cap, allows only static `display:none` cosmetics, and reduces the shield to a binary protected or paused indicator instead of a live count ([divergence register](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/spec/divergence-register.md)). Apple's newer `WKWebExtension` adds a second declarative path. It still provides no `webRequest`, so full uBlock Origin cannot come to iOS ([iOS port revisit](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-ios-port-revisit.md)). Meanwhile, uBlock Origin Lite reached Safari on iOS in August 2025 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/8/ublock-origin-lite-arrives-on-safari-with-built-in-filter-lists-and-youtube-ad-blocking/)), and DuckDuckGo turned on YouTube ad blocking by default on iOS in July 2026 ([adblock-tester](https://adblock-tester.com/ad-blockers/?p=1647)). On iPhone, "blocks ads by default" is now table stakes, not a pitch. The EU is the only exception for engines, and a Blink or Gecko build there would be exactly the fork-scale engine work the owner rejected on October 5.

Android is a different market. A Blanc for Android could intercept requests programmatically, as the desktop app does, so Blanc's blocking edge would survive there ([divergence register](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/spec/divergence-register.md)). But Brave already occupies that ground, and the indie Android scene is a graveyard of single-maintainer forks. Kiwi was archived in January 2025 and its extension code went into Edge ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/kiwi-browser-shutting-down-3519510/)), and Mull ended with DivestOS at the end of 2024 ([Codeberg](https://codeberg.org/librewolf/issues/issues/2169)). Blanc's desktop users also lean Mac: 136 of 239 recorded launches were on macOS ([growth spec](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-20-growth-counter-offensive-design.md)). iOS first is the right call, and Android should stay out of scope until iOS has proven itself.

## Indie iOS browsers win critics, not crowds, and each leaves a gap

Quiche is the benchmark, because it is what a one-person, craft-led iOS browser looks like when it succeeds. Gregory de Jonckheere, a French developer in Tokyo, shipped 1.0 in July 2023 ([Quiche press kit](https://quiche.industries/browser/press/)). It blocks ads, trackers and cookie banners by default, collects no analytics, needs no account, and lets users place every toolbar button ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quiche-browser/id1668363952)). Since version 1.72 it opens AI-free search results by default ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/07/16/quiche-browser-now-defaults-to-no-ai-web-search-results)). Its press is the envy of any indie: John Gruber called it "exquisitely designed" and "stunningly handsome" in March 2026 ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/03/20/quiche-browser)), and MacStories, 9to5Mac and Kottke have all covered it ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/11/indie-app-spotlight-quiche-customizable-iphone-web-browser/); [kottke.org](https://kottke.org/26/04/0048684-this-looks-interesting-quiche-is)). It ships about two updates a month, most recently 1.75 on October 18, 2026 ([What's New](https://quiche.industries/browser/whatsnew/)). Even so, after three years it shows about **4.9 stars from 834 US ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quiche-browser/id1668363952)), which points to a user base in the low tens of thousands at most. That is an inference, since no download or revenue figure exists for it. Quiche funds itself with Quiche PLUS, about $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year in the US, which unlocks cosmetic and comfort features while blocking stays free. Its most common criticism is that it has no sync, and its FAQ says that is deliberate ([Tsamoudakis](https://www.tsamoudakis.com/from-safari-to-quiche-discovering-the-browser-that-clicks/)).

The rest of the niche splits into funded "second browsers", single-purpose tools and monetised utilities. Kagi's Orion offers WebExtensions on iPhone and zero telemetry. Users report that "most extensions just don't work" and that it still "reads like a beta" ([digitbin](https://www.digitbin.com/chrome-extensions-on-iphone-orion-browser/)), it holds about 4.1 stars from roughly 1,000 ratings, and it isn't open source. Its Orion+ supporter tier ($5 a month, $50 a year or $150 lifetime) is the closest precedent for Blanc Patron ([Kagi](https://help.kagi.com/orion/orion-plus/orion-plus.html)). Vivaldi for iOS has end-to-end-encrypted desktop sync, but MacStories found its sync slow and its top-placed tabs hard to reach with a thumb ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/vivaldi-for-ios-is-not-delivering-on-its-promise-just-yet/)). Firefox Focus does one-tap erase well and nothing more ([apppricinglab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1055677337)). Aloha sells a VPN with billing that Trustpilot reviewers say is hard to cancel ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/alohabrowser.com)). The casualties show how small browsers usually die. Ghostery shut its standalone browser around December 2024 to focus on extensions ([Ghostery](https://www.ghostery.com/blog/ghostery-private-browser-discontinued)). Most telling of all, on October 2, 2026, Kagi ended Orion for Windows and Linux for lack of resources, despite more than **50,000 paying customers** across Search and Orion as of June 2025 ([Kagi blog](https://blog.kagi.com/update-orion-linux-windows); [Kagi feedback](https://kagifeedback.org/d/7289-june-9th-2025-celebrating-50k-customers)). Small browsers rarely die from a lack of users. They die when a maintainer runs out of bandwidth or the company refocuses.

| Browser | Maker | Desktop pairing and sync | Open source | AI stance | Funding | Rating snapshot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiche | Solo indie | None, by choice | No | AI-free search by default | Cosmetic subscription | 4.9 from 834 (US) |
| Orion | Kagi | macOS app (sync unconfirmed) | No | None advertised | Orion+ supporter tier | about 4.1 from about 1K |

Read the table for where the gaps are. Privacy with default blocking is the most crowded position on iOS, and customisable minimal chrome is Quiche's turf. No indie offers an open-source iOS browser paired with a desktop browser and encrypted sync. No one offers a command-pill model with slash commands and a quick switcher as the primary way to manage tabs. Spaces or workspaces on a phone exist only in Vivaldi's tab stacks. These gaps are where Blanc can stand.

## What users lack is continuity and calm tabs, not a better blocker

The best-documented mobile pain points match Blanc's strengths more than its weaknesses. The first is Safari's iOS 26 redesign. Its compact Liquid Glass layout moved All Tabs behind a "…" menu, shrank back and forward, and buried Tab Groups, prompting complaints on Apple's own forums and a PhoneArena verdict that "Safari on iOS 26 is a mess" ([Apple Discussions](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256149560); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Safari-on-iOS-26-is-a-mess-but-you-can-fix-some-of-it_id175463)). An Apple engineer traced a rendering bug in the Bottom and Compact layouts to a CSS blur ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/813733)). The regression was bad enough that one developer built a new browser, INK, because "iOS 26 broke my favorite feature" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45715169)). Tab hoarding is the second, quieter complaint. Safari caps each tab group at 500 tabs ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/838715/how-many-tabs-can-you-have-open-on-the-iphone)), and users report that at the cap it silently drops a tab without saying which one ([MacRumors forum](https://spy.macrumors.com/threads/ios-safari-500-tab-limit.2398523)). The evidence is anecdotal, but it describes a felt annoyance. A bottom-anchored, thumb-reachable pill that opens a searchable switcher, groups tabs and never loses one silently is a direct answer, and it is Blanc's Island model translated to a phone.

Continuity is the third pain point, and the one only a desktop-first indie can fix. Apple's Handoff from Firefox or Brave on a Mac opens Safari on the iPhone even when another browser is the default ([Mozilla Connect](https://connect.mozilla.org/t5/ideas/ios-macos-handoff-issue/idi-p/3907); [Brave Community](https://community.brave.com/t/handoff-wants-to-open-safari-on-ios-when-brave-window-is-opened-on-macos/185334)), so every third-party browser has to build its own send and resume. Fans of design-led desktop browsers ask for exactly this. In 2024, Arc users said they couldn't make Arc their default "everywhere" until Arc Search brought their Spaces and tabs to the phone ([r/ArcBrowser](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/ArcBrowser/comments/1aeitw6/i_dont_understand)). Zen has no mobile app and says its layout doesn't fit small screens ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1957695/why-people-are-switching-to-zen-web-browser)). Reviewers judged Vivaldi iOS chiefly on how slowly it synced ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/vivaldi-for-ios-is-not-delivering-on-its-promise-just-yet/)). Switching costs work the same way: 28% of people in a YouGov poll said they feel "locked in" to their browser, and saved history, bookmarks and passwords make them less willing to switch ([YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/42384-global-can-consumers-change-their-internet-browser)). An app that arrives already populated from the user's desktop removes that friction for Blanc's own users.

Blanc already built most of what continuity needs, and most of it is server-side. The v1 Sync Worker stores Favorites, settings and per-device open-tab snapshots as opaque ciphertext. The eight-character pairing code shipped in v1.36.0, and its spec says plainly that "the same code and route work" for mobile when it exists ([pairing spec](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-sync-device-pairing-design.md); [releases.json](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/site/src/data/releases.json)). The client-side work is a Swift implementation of the scrypt→HKDF→AES-256-GCM scheme that is byte-compatible with `sync-crypto.js`. On mobile, users judge sync by speed and reliability before anything else, so this compatibility work is the single most important engineering task in the product.

The weak spots deserve plain statement. Blanc's iPhone blocker could block YouTube ads only as well as declarative rules allow, its shield could not show a live count, and nothing in the record shows that the Island metaphor works one-handed, because no mobile usability research exists ([iOS port revisit](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-ios-port-revisit.md)). The demand evidence for a companion app is also thin: a handful of social posts asking for iPhone options, and users counting tabs "on both phone and laptop" ([growth log](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/marketing/social/growth-log.md)).

## The ROI case fails on revenue and survives only on strategy

Start with the desktop base, because a companion can only attach to users who already exist. The last committed measurement (August 20, 2026) recorded **43 monthly actives**, 21 weekly actives, **766** cumulative installer downloads, 18.5% July-to-August cohort retention (5 of 27 people), and 4 GitHub stars. The same document concluded that "product velocity is not the constraint; distribution is" ([growth spec](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-20-growth-counter-offensive-design.md)). GitHub now reports 10,992 installer-asset requests, but updater fetches inflate that figure. The 1,536 DMG requests are the cleanest proxy for new Mac downloads ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/releases)). Launch Week results, newer active-user counts and Patron sales are not in the repository and should be pulled from the `blanc-ping` Worker and Polar before any milestone below is set.

Revenue per user is close to zero in every channel. Opera, the best public benchmark, earns about **$2.46–$2.49 a year per user**, but two-thirds of that comes from advertising ([Opera FY2025](https://opera.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/opera-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-ahead)), which a privacy-first browser won't sell. The only public search partner rate is DuckDuckGo's 50/50 split for a default placement, and it dates from 2012 ([Parabola](https://lists.parabola.nu/pipermail/dev/2012-October/000911.html)). No partner publishes a traffic minimum, and none is likely to contract with a browser that has hundreds of users. Paid acquisition doesn't pay back: Apple Ads cost a median **$1.80 per install** ([AppTweak](https://apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)), against lifetime revenue per install measured in cents. Blanc Patron costs **US$30 a year or US$4 a month** and unlocks nothing ([Patron page](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/site/src/pages/patron.astro)), so it behaves like a tip jar. RevenueCat's trial-conversion medians of 25.5–42.5% describe paywalled apps, and a tip jar sits well below them ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). The good news is on cost: Apple's Small Business Program takes 15%, and Blanc already pays the $99 developer fee for desktop notarization, so the account costs nothing extra.

The real cost is maintainer time, and the internal yardstick is sobering. The September native-macOS audit estimated **4–6 months** for a first public Swift parity app, judged the ~3,000-line iOS prototype "not tester-grade", and warned that "every feature costs twice, permanently" ([Swift audit](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/native-macos-swift-audit.md)). The prototype was built July 7–10, has never reached TestFlight, has no CI, and has a start page broken since October 9. It targets a spec that has since grown from 24 to 51 features ([iOS port revisit](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-ios-port-revisit.md)).

The model below runs twelve months after an iOS launch. Every input is an assumption, labeled as such, and should be replaced with Blanc's own data once it is pulled.

Read as returns, the model says direct revenue covers almost none of the effort. It comes to roughly **$3, $65 and $3,100 per maintainer-month** across the three cases. Only the high case, which needs Quiche-level press and a search partner who signs, starts to resemble pay. A break-even check makes the scale concrete. Suppose the ongoing 20% of the maintainer's time is valued at a notional $5,000 per maintainer-month (an assumption chosen only to size the problem). Funding it from Patron alone takes about $12,000 a year, which is about 470 payers, which at 1% conversion means about **47,000 iOS monthly actives**. That is probably more people than Quiche has reached after three years of excellent press. Grants change the picture more than users do: one €15K–€50K award would exceed several years of plausible direct revenue. But NLnet's NGI Zero Commons Fund closed its final call on June 1, 2026 ([NLnet](https://www.nlnet.nl/commonsfund)), OTF focuses on at-risk users and censorship circumvention ([GC4HR](https://www.gc4hr.org/resource/internet-freedom-fund-otf/)), and both of Blanc's grant drafts, NLnet and FUTO, are unsubmitted and scope desktop work only ([NLnet draft](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/grants/nlnet-commons-fund.md); [FUTO draft](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/grants/futo-pitch.md)).

The steelman for proceeding rests on value outside the revenue line. The App Store is a discovery channel that desktop Blanc lacks, and the August diagnosis named distribution as the binding constraint. A good iOS app can earn enthusiast press, as Quiche shows. A phone app answers the most common reason design-led desktop browsers lose users, and it could raise desktop retention among people who pair a phone. That effect is unproven: the only evidence is Branch's vendor claim that cross-platform users spend 2.2x more ([Branch](https://www.branch.io/resources/blog/cross-platform-mobile-app-trends/)). Testing it means comparing retention for Sync users against non-Sync users. In the EU, Apple lists a browser on the choice screen once it has at least **5,000 EU iPhone downloads** in the prior year ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/support/browser-choice-screen)). Apple shows the 12 most popular eligible browsers per country, so clearing the threshold is necessary but not sufficient ([Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/apple-details-updated-browser-choice-screen-for-ios-and-ipados-in-europe/)). Being listed is valuable. Firefox estimates its EU iOS daily users ran about **113%** above the counterfactual after the screens arrived, and users who pick Firefox there stay about five times longer than organic downloads ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/05/12/eu-law-bestows-6m-more-firefox-users-upon-us-moz-says/5238623); [NBER w35112](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35112/w35112.pdf)). The counterweight is opportunity cost. Base-case year one spends about half a maintainer-year on iOS while desktop has, at last count, dozens of active users.

## Scope it thin, position it as "your Blanc, in your pocket"

The best-returning positioning is a continuity-first companion, with calm, one-handed chrome as the reason to keep it. Concretely: pair an iPhone with the shipped code, see your desktop's open tabs under Other devices, keep your Favorites and groups, send a tab back to the Mac, and browse with blocking on by default and no AI, in an MIT-licensed app. The secondary pitch is the Island as a bottom pill. Its Quick Switcher, a core set of slash commands and Bangs give phone users a predictable alternative to Safari's iOS 26 layout. Blanc should not claim to out-block Brave or DuckDuckGo, should not lead with "privacy browser" (the most crowded spot on iOS), and should run every public line past `docs/marketing-claims.md`, which treats an unreleased build as proving nothing ([marketing claims](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/marketing-claims.md)).

Version 1.0 should stop well short of the July roadmap's M7–M16 parity arc ([iOS roadmap](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-07-ios-port-roadmap-design.md)). Ship iPhone only, with a raised OS floor as the revisit proposes. Include the bottom pill with the switcher and a small command set, `WKContentRuleList` blocking from the existing generated artifact with the binary shield, private tabs, Favorites, the Sync client with pairing, Other devices, send tab, and read-only display of desktop groups. Leave iPad, Android, Spaces editing, StoreKit Patron and the full filter pipeline for later, each gated on the indicators below. Deferring StoreKit costs almost nothing, because base-case Patron revenue is a few hundred dollars. It also sidesteps the unresolved US link-out question and the stale D5 text, which still says Patron unlocks Spaces ([divergence register](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/spec/divergence-register.md)). Two logistics items have lead times and should start now: request the default-browser entitlement, and add an iOS CI job so the prototype stops rotting while desktop moves on ([iOS port revisit](https://github.com/bnfy/blanc/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-ios-port-revisit.md)). On the cost side, sync servers add little. Filter-list refreshes either ride App Review or need a signed download that stays consistent with Blanc's "no live filter-list request at startup" posture. That design decision belongs in the M6.5 re-baseline.

The indicators below are thresholds the owner sets in advance, not industry benchmarks. No sourced mobile retention benchmark for indie browsers exists, and desktop's only cohort figure rests on 27 people. Measuring them on iOS needs opt-in, aggregate telemetry consistent with desktop's design: install-scoped, private tabs excluded, and nothing joined to Sync data.

| Stage | Indicator | Keep going | Kill or rethink trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before build (desktop) | Clicks on a "Blanc on iPhone" waitlist on the start page and site | ≥100 sign-ups, or ≥10% of Mac actives, within 6 weeks | <25 sign-ups: scope down to a sync viewer, or pause |
| TestFlight beta | Day-7 return among testers | ≥30% | <15% after two beta builds |
| Launch +60 days | Share of desktop Sync users who pair an iPhone | ≥15% | <5%: the continuity premise fails |
| Launch +90 days | Day-30 retention (opt-in signal, like desktop's `day1_*`) | ≥15% | <8% for two consecutive monthly cohorts |
| Launch +6 months | iOS monthly actives | ≥750, on the base-case path | <200 |
| Launch +6 months | App Store reputation and press | ≥4.5 stars from ≥100 ratings; one enthusiast review | <4.0, or no organic coverage |
| Launch +12 months | EU downloads, trailing 12 months | Approaching the 5,000 choice-screen threshold | — (upside only) |
| Ongoing guardrail | iOS share of maintainer time; desktop release health | ≤25% of time; desktop cadence and gates intact | >35% of time for two quarters, or a skipped desktop gate |
| Ongoing | 90-day desktop retention, paired vs unpaired users | Paired users clearly higher | No difference after two quarters: the strategic case weakens |

The kill rule should be explicit. At the six-month review, if any two of the triggers above have fired, the app moves to maintenance-only (OS-compatibility fixes, no features) or a clean, announced sunset, as Ghostery did. It should not stagnate quietly, the way Kiwi and Mull's users lived through before their shutdowns.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Whether a mobile Blanc can win share is the wrong frame, because no indie browser wins share on iOS, and Quiche's acclaim with a few hundred US ratings proves it. The useful question is whether a small iPhone companion makes desktop Blanc stickier and more discoverable at a time cost the maintainer can sustain. That question is cheap to test and has clear thresholds. The model shows that revenue will not justify the work at any plausible user count, so success has to be judged on attach, retention and desktop health, with grants as the only realistic money. That makes a mobile-scoped grant application, written alongside the M6.5 re-baseline, the most valuable non-code task in the plan.

The less obvious implication is that the iOS app's moat is desktop work Blanc has already done: the encrypted Sync Worker, the pairing code, the MIT licence and the AI-free rule. Each of these is something Quiche refuses, Orion lacks or the funded players have abandoned. The danger is the reverse of the usual startup failure. Blanc will not die from too few users. Like Orion on Windows and Linux, it is at risk from a second platform quietly eating the maintainer's one scarce resource, and the guardrail on maintainer time matters more than any growth target.
