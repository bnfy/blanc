## The bug

On v1.47.0, a page with a frame that failed its certificate check ended the
whole browser with SIGSEGV on the main thread. The report was from Arch Linux,
on the BadSSL dashboard. It reproduced on the published v1.47.0 Mac app.

Electron 45 rewrote `App::AllowCertificateError` to emit `certificate-error` on
the WebContents and then on `app`, with the same `const GURL&`. Blanc's
WebContents listener answered synchronously, and the second emit then read
freed memory: `strlen` < `v8::String::NewFromUtf8` <
`gin::Converter<std::string>::ToV8` < `EmitEvent` <
`App::AllowCertificateError`, symbolized with Electron's published symbols.
That the freed value is the URL is inferred from the stack and source.

A minimal Electron app with no Blanc code crashed on stock 45.2.1 (macOS and
Linux ARM64) and didn't on 44.9.3 (macOS). Bad-certificate pages opened directly
didn't crash, and why they survive is unknown. The code was unchanged on
Electron's `main` and `45-x-y` on this date, so this is a workaround in Blanc.

## Checks on the fix

- `@F39-8` failed before the fix (the browser died) and passes after. Allowing
  every certificate made it fail, so it does detect a frame that loads.
- The BadSSL dashboard for 30 s with the fixed source, unpacked, on stock 45.2.1
  and on `45.2.1+wvcus`: no crash through 41 and 46 certificate failures.
- The published Apple Silicon v1.47.1 app, from the DMG on a throwaway profile,
  on the BadSSL dashboard for 40 s: no crash through 43 certificate failures, and
  no new crash report.
- **Not checked:** Blanc with the fix on Linux or Windows, and the x64 AppImage
  the report was about. A minimal app on Linux ARM64 that leaves refusals
  unanswered, as the fix does, didn't crash, with the OS sandbox off. "Continue
  anyway" inside a frame can't be reached by a test, because Chromium remembers
  a continued certificate and doesn't ask Blanc again.
