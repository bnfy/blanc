# 5:2 header brief — Your browser's business model is part of the browser

Status: **concept brief only**. No image has been generated, uploaded, or
published. The article copy and final creative still require approval.

Target artboard: **2000×800 px** (5:2), with a 160 px outer safe area and no
critical detail in the outermost 10% of either side.

## Creative objective

Make the article's argument understandable before someone reads the first
paragraph:

> A browser can answer to an advertising system, or it can answer more directly
> to the people who use and support it.

This is an editorial statement about incentive structures, not a claim that a
specific browser secretly sells browsing history. The image must not depict
URLs, messages, passwords, histories, or other personal data flowing to an
advertiser.

## Recommended direction — Who does the browser answer to?

Create a restrained black-and-white editorial diagram on a soft-paper field.
The panorama is divided into two balanced systems, separated by generous white
space rather than a hard split.

### Left system

- A neutral browser-window outline sits between a small human figure and three
  institutional labels: `advertising`, `measurement`, and `shareholders`.
- Fine black lines connect the browser to the three labels.
- The lines should read as accountability and commercial relationships—not as
  stolen-data pipes.
- A small mono eyebrow reads `the platform model`.

### Right system

- A second neutral browser-window outline sits between a small human figure and
  one label: `the people using it`.
- One direct black line connects the person and the browser.
- A small mono eyebrow reads `the independent model`.
- Do not put a price, Patron badge, or subscription symbol in the cover; the
  article explains the actual Blanc model with the needed qualifications.

### Center headline

Use one compact, centered sentence-case line spanning the visual relationship:

`Who does your browser answer to?`

Optional supporting line, only if the diagram remains immediately legible:

`The business model shapes the product.`

The headline uses the Blanc website hierarchy: UI sans, approximately 500
weight, restrained negative tracking, and compact line height. The support line
is regular weight and muted gray. No all-caps title.

## Visual rules

- Palette: `#ffffff`, `#f7f7f7`, `#dedede`, `#111111`, and `#6b6b6b` only.
- No accent colors, gradients, photography, textures, neon glows, or fake
  browser controls.
- Use simple editorial geometry rather than a generated 3D scene.
- If the Blanc mark appears, it must be a black mark on an uninterrupted white
  field with clear space. The preferred cover omits the mark because the
  publication already supplies brand context.
- Keep every label legible at approximately 1000×400 display size.
- Preserve the exact 5:2 composition in both Substack and X Article crops;
  platform-native overlays must not cover the headline or labels.

## Rejected directions

### Big Brother eye

A giant eye, surveillance camera, or person staring through the browser is too
literal and would imply specific monitoring behavior the article does not
claim. It also turns the essay into fear advertising.

### Google-versus-Blanc logo fight

Side-by-side corporate logos would make the cover read as an attack ad and
would require trademark handling. The essay is about incentive structures,
not a winner-versus-loser comparison.

### Coins pouring out of browsing history

This would visually assert that browsing history is sold. The cited sources do
not support that claim, so the concept fails the marketing-claim gate.

### Generic padlock

A lock would frame the piece as a narrow security article and obscure the
business-model thesis.

## Production and review checklist

1. Render at 2000×800 and export PNG plus an editable source.
2. Inspect the full frame and a centered 1600×640 crop.
3. Confirm the title is sentence case and visually centered on the actual
   artboard.
4. Confirm no line or label implies a personal-data flow.
5. Confirm the Blanc mark is absent or strictly black on white.
6. Confirm all text remains readable at 1000×400.
7. Review alongside the article title so the image adds meaning rather than
   merely repeating it.
8. Obtain creative approval before generating the final asset, then obtain a
   separate action-time approval before upload or publication.
