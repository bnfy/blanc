# Blanc voice and formatting canon

Status: Working standard for drafting and reviewing Blanc marketing. This file
does not authorize publication or changes to live accounts.

## Core voice

Blanc should sound clear, curious, independent, and human. The writing can be
casual without looking careless, and confident without sounding like a generic
brand campaign. Lead with the tension a person recognizes, explain why it
matters to them, and then show the release-backed product decision that responds
to it.

Tone and capitalization are separate choices. Blanc's personality does not
depend on writing everything in lowercase.

## Choose the register for the context

Use normal sentence capitalization and punctuation as the safe default, then
adjust deliberately for the format and audience. Proper writing is required
when Blanc needs to communicate authority, trust, precision, or permanence,
including:

- launch announcements and substantive product education;
- articles, essays, release notes, and longer product explanations;
- creator, press, partnership, and Ambassador outreach;
- privacy, security, licensing, telemetry, and policy discussions;
- substantive replies where clarity or credibility matters;
- application forms, program terms, and calls for participation;
- image and video headlines unless a deliberately casual concept calls for a
  different treatment.

Capitalize Blanc, named product features, platforms, people, organizations,
acronyms, and the beginning of sentences correctly in this register. Do not
force fragments when a complete sentence reads better.

For ordinary owned social posts, choose the register post by post. A polished
product explanation will usually use sentence case. A quick observation, meme,
casual question, or culturally fluent reply may use lowercase. Neither style is
mandatory merely because the content appears on social media.

## Selective lowercase

Lowercase is an optional creative device for a short, conversational social
hook, punchline, meme caption, or brief reply. Use it only when it makes the
line feel more natural for that specific platform and conversation. It should
not carry across every paragraph, CTA, graphic, or channel by default.

Do not treat “social” as synonymous with “lowercase.” A polished owned post can
use normal capitalization while still sounding candid, independent, and
slightly against the grain. Lowercase may cover one intentional hook, a very
short casual post, or a punchline without becoming a blanket rule for every
caption, paragraph, CTA, graphic, or channel.

Avoid lowercase when it could make Blanc look careless, evasive, unserious, or
less credible. If there is no clear benefit to the lowercase treatment, use
standard sentence case. Conversely, do not make a playful reply stiff or
corporate solely to satisfy a capitalization rule.

## Display type

Use sentence case for titles and headlines. Avoid full all-caps headlines.
Uppercase remains acceptable for very small utility labels, compact metadata,
and established acronyms such as POV, AI, URL, and X when the treatment matches
the design.

## Practical test

Before approving copy, ask:

1. Does the casing fit the context, or is it merely repeating a brand habit?
2. Would the same wording still sound like Blanc with normal capitalization?
3. Does the audience need warmth and informality here, or credibility and
   precision?
4. Is the emotional benefit clear before the feature description?
5. Is every material product claim backed by the current public release?

When in doubt, choose proper writing conventions. Then preserve Blanc's
character through the idea, rhythm, and point of view—not casing alone.

## Examples

Professional product post:

> Chrome now lets people choose whether the tab strip sits on top or down the
> side. Blanc starts with a different question: Why should the tab list stay
> visible at all?

Casual reply:

> four campaigns and only four tabs? honestly that sounds like elite containment

Mixed treatment:

> keep the tabs. lose the strip.
>
> See why Blanc puts the page back at the center of your browser.
