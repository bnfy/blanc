Status: Staged as the follow-up unit after the Ambassador launch. Nothing in

## Why this unit exists

Chrome's current horizontal-or-vertical choice creates a relevant browser
design tension without requiring Blanc to attack another product. The user
benefit comes first: moving a persistent list may not make a crowded browser
feel less crowded. Blanc responds by keeping the list inside the Island until
the user opens it.

## Substack Note

> Chrome now offers a choice between horizontal and vertical tabs. That is
> useful—but it still assumes the full tab list should remain on screen.
>
> Blanc starts with a different question: Why should the list stay visible
> when you are not using it?
>
> If a busy browser already feels crowded, moving the list may not change the
> feeling. Blanc keeps tabs within reach inside the Island, so the page stays
> in front. Open the Island when you need the list; dismiss it when you do not.
>
> This is not a claim that one layout is right for everybody. It is a different
> tradeoff for people who want the webpage—not the browser interface—to remain
> the visual center.
