Quiet Tabs

A tab left idle has its renderer discarded to reclaim memory, and rebuilt when you return to it. A quiet tab is shown in exactly one way: its whole row dims to half, on the ⌘L panel and the vertical rail. Hover or focus the row and it returns to full strength — it is about to wake. There is no glyph, tag or word; the fade alone is the marker (an earlier build drew a Zzz pictogram and a "quiet" label, removed 2026-08-18 because they repeated down every restored row and read as junk). Pill dots and the Quick Switcher carry no quiet state. Every visible and assistive string still says quiet — asleep is the internal field name only.

Why it looks like this

The fade is the whole marker.

Quiet dims the entire row to half on the panel and the rail, and nowhere else. An earlier build drew a Zzz glyph and a "quiet" tag at rest; on a freshly restored session those repeated down every row and read as junk, so they were removed 2026-08-18. That a fully-restored list dims uniformly is a known, accepted trade-off — waking is transparent.

Restore on hover or focus.

Touch a quiet row and it returns to full strength: it is about to wake, and its pin, mute and close actions should read at full contrast while in use. The transition is a 120ms fade, matched on both surfaces.

Not the pill dots or the Quick Switcher.

Pill dots are switch targets, not status fields — nothing legible fits at 6px — and the Quick Switcher sub line is search text. A quiet mark on either added clutter without value, so neither carries one.

The word is "quiet".

Every visible and assistive string says quiet — the dim carries no text, so the accessible name is what announces the state to anyone who cannot see it. The tab record's field is asleep (internal only); the command is /sleep, the one deliberate place the internal word is shown to users — the opposite of the bookmarks split, where an identifier no user ever sees. A test fails the build if asleep reaches a user-facing string.
