Quiet Tabs

A tab left idle has its renderer discarded to reclaim memory, and rebuilt when you return to it. Quiet is shown in exactly one way, on two surfaces: the panel row and the vertical rail each draw a Zzz glyph and dim the favicon. Nowhere else — pill dots and the Quick Switcher carry no quiet state. The word a person sees or hears is always quiet — asleep is the internal field name only; the Zzz is a pictogram, not a string.

Why it looks like this

One treatment, two surfaces.

Quiet is the Zzz glyph plus a dimmed favicon, drawn on the panel row and the vertical rail, and nowhere else. Pill dots are switch targets, not status fields — nothing legible fits at 6px — and the Quick Switcher sub line is search text. A quiet mark on either added clutter without value, so neither carries one.

Dim the favicon, not the title.

The favicon is the whole visual unit that dims (it also holds the mute badge), so a quiet tab fades as one object. The title is left alone: loading already dims it, and the title span is aria-hidden — the favicon is what you scan.

The word is “quiet”.

Every visible and assistive string says quiet. The tab record's field is asleep (internal only); the command is /sleep, the one deliberate place where the internal word is shown to users — the opposite of the bookmarks split, where an identifier no user ever sees. A test fails the build if asleep reaches a user-facing string.
